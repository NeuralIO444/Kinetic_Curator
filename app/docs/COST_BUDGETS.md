## Worked example — grid-heavy-count-scale, the heaviest recipe in the corpus

The heaviest measured recipe is **grid-heavy-count-scale**: 480 bodies × large marks (scale
1.8–2.6), *no FX wraps, no cast*. Label **28,305.8 ms** in this environment — 19.87× the base
recipe, 3,538× the desktop cap and 5,661× the iPad cap (software-GL multiples; the portable
numbers are the 19.87× spread and the ordering). Walking it through the governor's answers, in
the order the governor would reach for them:

1. **The effect shed ladder finds almost nothing.** The declared-tier shed order
   (`costRegistry.mjs`: tier 1 before 2 before 3, costliest first, tier 0 never sheds) governs
   *effects* — and this recipe carries none of the sheddable kind: no spectrum marks (the
   tier-1 item), no FX stack. Its cost is not an effect; it is bodies × mark area, raw raster.
2. **The FX axis is not the lever either — the corpus proves it.** grid-heavy-all takes this
   same count × scale load and *adds* a cast of 24 plus 4 FX wraps, and measures slightly
   *less* (19.13×): at this load the wraps are lost in the raster cost. The dose series agrees
   everywhere — FX-1→4 at base scale run 1.16×/1.35×/1.38×/1.58×, 4 wraps on 480 small bodies
   add ~11%, on large marks ~5%. This is the measured version of why #192 retired FX count as
   a budget: the GPU composites the wraps; it is the marks that cost.
3. **What answers this bust is the count ceiling ladder.** The quality tiers
   (`QUALITY_PRESETS`) cap bodies at FULL 800 → SHOW 420 → LEAN 180 (LEAN also drops mirror and
   gloss). The count series prices a step: 480 bodies measure 4.96× base, 160 measure 2.54× —
   roughly halving bodies halves this component. Scale is the performer's dial, not a shed
   axis; the governor does not reach into it. So the honest shed story for the corpus's worst
   recipe is one sentence: **fewer bodies, in ceiling steps** — there is nothing cheaper to
   take first, because the expensive thing is the picture itself.
4. **The two axes multiply, but not cleanly.** Count alone is 4.96× and scale alone is 7.09×;
   independent axes would predict ~35× together. Measured: 19.87×. Fixed costs are shared, so
   the corpus punishes the *combination* less than the product of its parts — good news for the
   governor, whose ceilings only ever pull one axis at a time.

On device hardware this recipe is the first one the caps will judge, and the ordering says it
should still be the corpus's problem case: count × scale is the dominant cost axis on every
environment the harness has measured. When the Studio dataset lands (#1578's remaining run),
this same report regenerated from it gives the device-truth verdicts — and if grid-heavy-count-scale
fits the iPad's 5.0 ms there, the corpus's whole grid axis fits with it.
