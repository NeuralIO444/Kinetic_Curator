# The Kinetic_Curator Manifesto

*Teenage Engineering builds the instrument. Joshua Davis writes the system. Neri Oxman grows the living medium. September 2026.*

An art teacher's advice: develop something new by combining the work of three artists — never one, never a copy. Davis never published a manifesto. He published systems. This is ours, distilled from thirty years of his practice, braced by two more disciplines, and written down so the work order, the governor, and every PR can point at it.

## 0. The thesis

**The governor is the curator.** Not a safety valve — a taste. When resources run short, it decides what survives, guided by a taste model trained on kept renders, enforcing deliberate limitations. Teenage Engineering builds the instrument; Davis tells you what it's for: program the rules, let the system surprise you, freeze the beautiful moment. Oxman tells you what it becomes: don't manufacture the image — grow it.

## The seven principles

**1. The code is the artwork.** Creativity lives in the system behind the images, not in any single output. The swarm bake, the ACCUM passes, the FX layers — those are the art. Renders are specimens.

**2. Controlled chaos.** Set the rules — the palette, the physics, the boundaries — then let the system surprise you. Same look and feel every time, never the same image twice. Seeded, replayable, honest chaos: run it again and the moment comes back.

**3. The artist is the curator.** Run the system hundreds of times, freeze the keeper, throw the rest away. Taste is selection. The keep/pass ledger and the taste model are this principle as software — and now the governor curates too, in real time, on stage.

**4. Constraints are the aesthetic.** The tornado only does what you told it to. Four panels. Declared cost tiers. A documented shed order. Limitations shown honestly are a feature of the instrument, not an apology.

**5. Make the tools your own.** When the new machine arrived, Davis didn't reject it — he trained it on his own work. The taste model learns from your keeps only. Never clicks, never anyone else's renders. The instrument carries your hand.

**6. Share the code.** MIT. Open source. Software by creatives, for creatives.

**7. Grow, don't manufacture.** Manufacturing stamps the same image every time. Growing sets initial conditions and tends what emerges. The instrument favors systems with a temporal dimension: trails that accumulate like sediment, marks that weather over a performance, voices that evolve across a set. A render is not printed; it is cultivated — then kept.

## The third pillar

Two pillars were load-bearing from the start: TE's playful industrialism (the instrument, the enforcer — *"what can we afford?"*) and Davis's dynamic abstraction (the system, the curator — *"what's worth keeping?"*). The adversarial pair stays. It drives the curator engine.

The third pillar is the medium they argue over. Oxman's material ecology — growing versus manufacturing, gradients instead of seams, systems that behave like organisms — translates into real-time digital work as a single demand: **the image must be alive in time.** Static stamps are out, whatever their pedigree. When the enforcer and the curator agree, the work grows.

The translation is honest about its limits: Oxman's work is physical, biological, and slow; ours is digital and runs at sixty frames a second. We take the principle — gradient materiality, growth over assembly, the organism as co-author — not the medium.

## The governor as curator

The governor already sheds load in a documented order. The manifesto promotes it: every shed is a curatorial decision about what matters most.

- **MLX thinks offline; the instrument performs cheap.** Taste training, cost modeling, semantic golden tests — all heavy thinking happens on the Mac Studio. The live path enforces from exported artifacts: `taste.json`, measured cost tiers, golden embeddings. Bake heavy, evaluate cheap. No model on the frame loop.
- **Deliberate limitations, performed.** The governor doesn't dim the show and apologize. The constraint readout in PLAY says what's shed and why, and recovers when headroom returns. The limits are visible, honest, and part of the music.
- **The shed order is the taste, written down.** Resolution, quality, assets, counts, motion — that priority is a curatorial statement. It changes only deliberately, the way a tracklist changes.

## What this demands of every PR

The standing gate, restated: every change serves **perform, capture, learn, or guide** — or it doesn't ship. The manifesto adds two questions: *does it serve the instrument or the ego?* A feature that makes the system more surprising inside its limits ships. A feature that makes the limits fuzzier doesn't. And the Oxman question: *does it grow, or does it stamp?* A system with a temporal dimension ships; a static stamp doesn't.
