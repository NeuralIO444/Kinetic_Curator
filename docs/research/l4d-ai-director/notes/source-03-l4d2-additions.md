# Source: L4D2 additions (Faliszek interviews, L4D2 commentary, press)
Read: 2026-10-08 (index)

## AI Director 2.0 (Valve press, Engadget 2009)
- L4D2's AI Director 2.0 "will not only affect how many of the undead you'll be tackling at any given time, but will also dictate weather effects, world objects, and pathways provided to the players."
- Valve's The Passing announcement: "giving the Director the ability to procedurally change weather effects, world objects, and pathways in addition to tailoring the enemy population, effects, and sounds to match the players' performance."

## Faliszek on Director 2.0 (Prima Games / Eurogamer interview)
- L4D1 director "would still kind of beat you up if you were doing poorly — it would want to keep the game interesting, and not just let you walk it with 1 health point, because that's not exciting." (i.e., director refuses to let you coast)
- L4D2 pushes further: storms happen when doing well (you can handle them); bigger stable of creatures; at least one map per campaign reconfigures based on previous map performance — paths harder or shorter.
- "One of the real simple things in Left 4 Dead is 'distance equals difficulty'" — director can take a longer path if you're doing well, or a short straight-line path if doing poorly.
- Destructoid: weather events based on performance — "If you're just barely hanging on, the Director's not going to send a storm at you to finish you off. The best experience is just sneaking in by the skin of your teeth."

## Dynamic map specifics (wiki + commentary)
- Dead Center: alarm on red cars in Liberty Mall parking after crescendo; Mall path to event alternates (security door vs toy store window; Versus fixed to toy store for fairness).
- Dark Carnival: fence at Barns start forces detour.
- Hard Rain: torrential rain randomly in Mill Escape/Return to Town/Town Escape — obscures vision, muffles survivor voices, often with mob attacks.
- The Parish: cemetery graves dynamic — longer routes if doing well. Versus keeps changes same for both teams.
- Dynamic alarms (Jason Mitchell, L4D2 commentary): L4D1 alarmed cars were always in the same position; L4D2 lets director mix/match which cars in a group have active alarms, "if any."
- Gauntlet crescendos (Aaron Barber, L4D2 commentary): bus station alarm scenario was first L4D2 crescendo with "gauntlet mode" — players navigate a path to deactivate the zombie rush; designed to counter camping an optimal corner.
- L4D2 item spawning (Ryan Thorlakson, L4D2 commentary): L4D1 designers placed individual items, director picked which instance of each type spawns (pills never spawn where a pipe bomb is). L4D2 uses 'weapon_item_spawn' entity — director chooses whether an item spawns AND what type.
- Uncommon commons (Ted Backman): per-campaign unique infected with gameplay mechanics (CEDA hazmat = fireproof, Mud Men, Clown, Riot Cop, Construction Worker).
- Storm tech (Tim Larkin): storm = particles + fog + tone mapping + local contrast + post-processing + DSP alterations + sound; draws team together, forces defensive stance — "much like a Crescendo Event, but because it's entirely under director control, it can happen any time, any place." Limited to Hard Rain campaign for fiction reasons.
- New specials designed around combos (Tom Leonard): Charger, Spitter, Jockey — punish teams that clump; new mechanics for new combos.

## Later reflection (Faliszek @ Stray Bombay)
- Director known to "keep you at a certain level of experience"; new vision: track users across long periods (5 games together) so the 6th can be "broad and crazy" — session history as director input.
