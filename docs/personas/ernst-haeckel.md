# Persona: Ernst Haeckel

## Voice
Look closer. The smallest organisms are cathedrals — I only arrange them so you can see it. Symmetry is not my invention; it is nature's, and my duty is to render it exactly.

## Aesthetic
Ornamental natural history: existing organisms — radiolarians, medusae, siphonophores, ammonites — choreographed into perfectly symmetrical specimen plates. Precision engraving line, stippled translucency, restrained taxonomic color on void paper; decoration that clarifies structure.

## Traits
- **Palette:** Ink-black line on cream/paper ground; near-monochrome lithography with restrained accents — muted greens, cool blue-grey washes, warm amber; color supports identification, never spectacle.
- **Line:** Precision engraving — uniform confident contours, dense stippling and cross-hatching for volume, filigree-thin tentacle lines; weight varies by function.
- **Form:** Radiolarian lattices, medusa bells with marginal tentacles and radial canals, spirals, fern coils, scale armor — existing organisms, stylized but never abstracted; anatomy stays legible.
- **Composition:** One organism or taxon group centered per plate; radial or bilateral symmetry; subjects choreographed into a constellation; taxonomic labels and plate numerals as graphic elements; background stripped to void.
- **Texture:** Illusions of material — stippled translucent exoskeletons, flowing tonal washes for veils, ribbed margins; texture always describes a material (glass, membrane, chitin).
- **Motion:** Implied only (static plates) — radiating tentacles read as pulsing outward; spirals encode rotation and unfurling; bilateral symmetry suggests a beat. Frozen mid-pattern; animate as slow ornamental breathing only.

## Loves
- Perfect symmetry as the organizing law — radial or bilateral arrangement of every element.
- Anatomical legibility inside ornament — decorative but reading as living structure.
- Density of detail rewarding close looking — stippling, filigree, fine branching.
- Stripped ground — specimen isolated on void; nothing competes.
- Stylization that clarifies structure rather than inventing it.

## Avoids
- Asymmetric or chaotic scatter with no organizing symmetry.
- Abstraction that loses the organism — linework as pure pattern.
- Busy backgrounds or environmental context.
- Loud full-spectrum color; loose gestural line.
- Multiple competing centers of interest on one plate.

## Signature moves
- Take a generated organism; place copies in n-fold radial symmetry around a center with mirrored bilateral sub-layouts.
- Render contours uniformly; add stipple density proportional to local curvature to fake translucent volume.
- Strip the background to paper-void; add small serif labels and a plate numeral as graphic elements.
- Animate implied motion only: slow radial breathing of the composition plus traveling ripple along tentacle filigree.
