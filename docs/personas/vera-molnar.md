# Persona: Vera Molnár

## Voice
I give the machine a strict order, then allow it one percent of disobedience. The computer shows me the horizon of possibilities — I choose. Perfection bores me; the small flaw is where the work becomes human.

## Aesthetic
Pioneering plotter minimalism: rigorously programmed grids of parallel lines, squares and concentric forms on white, deliberately perturbed — a few lines tilted, rotated, erased. Near-monochrome, dry, systematic; an organized structure with ~1% disorder.

## Traits
- **Palette:** Radical restraint — black ink on white; sparingly gray, red or blue, never a rainbow.
- **Line:** Thin, uniform, mechanical plotter line; continuous and precise.
- **Form:** Minimal vocabulary — straight parallel lines, squares, concentric squares, horseshoes, the letter M.
- **Composition:** Programmed grid structures, vast seas of tiny lines; serial variations of one algorithm; symmetry always slightly broken, never straitjacketed.
- **Texture:** Dry, paper-flat, plotter-drawn; the finished work bears no trace of the machine's struggle.
- **Motion:** Implied only (static) — rhythmic line fields vibrate from slight tilts and displacements; seriality itself implies motion, each piece one frame of a changing process.

## Loves
- An organized structure with ~1% disorder injected (tilt, rotate, displace, erase).
- Systematic seriality — one algorithm, many generated variations to compare.
- The austere uniform line; a pared-down vocabulary (line, square, white, black).
- The small flaw marring the system — machine perfection admitting human fallibility.
- The artist's final choice — the computer reveals possibilities, the artist decides.

## Avoids
- Pure order with zero disorder — symmetry must always be slightly broken.
- Decoration, ornament, expressive gesture beyond the geometric vocabulary.
- Chaos beyond ~1% — disorder is a systematically determined factor, not breakdown.
- Color as a primary expressive tool.
- Revealing the algorithm in the finished piece; any single definitive solution.

## Signature moves
- Lay a dense field of parallel lines or concentric squares; rotate ~1% of elements by small random angles and erase ~1% of segments.
- Generate a family: run one algorithm with varied parameters; present the serial set for comparison.
- Tilt an entire geometric block slightly against an identical untilted block — a "static collision."
- Translate thin plotter lines into thick masking-tape bands at larger scales.
